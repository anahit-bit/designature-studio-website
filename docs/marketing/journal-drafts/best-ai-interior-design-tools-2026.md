## Body

### The short version

AI interior design has split into three kinds of tool, and picking the right one starts with knowing which kind you actually need:

1. **One-click render tools** — upload a photo, get a restyled room in seconds. Great for ideas, but the furniture usually isn't real and there's no one to help.
2. **Shoppable AI tools** — the redesign comes with a list of real products you can buy. Better for actually finishing a room.
3. **AI + a human designer** — the AI gets you started fast and a real designer refines it and keeps you on track. Best when the room matters and you don't want to go it alone.

Below, the tools worth knowing in 2026 — with what each is honestly best for.

### How we compared them

We looked at five things a real person actually cares about: **quality of the redesign**, whether it keeps *your* room's real proportions (windows, ceiling, layout) instead of inventing a new one, whether the furniture is **real and shoppable**, whether there's a **human** in the loop, and **price**. We include tools we don't run ourselves and say plainly where they're stronger.

### The tools, one by one

**Designature Studio — best if you want AI speed and a real designer.**
You upload a photo, our AI Vision tool redesigns the room, a Style Quiz points it at your taste, and you get a curated Shopping List of real products. If you want more than a picture, a designer picks it up from there — a $99 consultation that's creditable toward a full project. It's the middle ground most tools skip: faster and cheaper than hiring a designer cold, more finished and more human than a render toy. *Best for:* people who want a beautiful result they can actually buy and build, with an expert on call.

**MeltFlex AI — best pure shoppable redesign.**
A slick tool that places real, priced furniture into your redesign and compares prices across several US stores. Genuinely useful for planning a purchase. Its weak spots (per public reviews) are consistency — it can invent doors or windows — and there's no human to sanity-check the result. *Best for:* a confident DIY-er who just wants a shoppable starting point.

**REimagine Home — best for real-estate staging.**
Strong at staging vacant listings and redesigning rooms, with a shoppable mode that links real products. Built more for agents than homeowners, and there's no designer. *Best for:* realtors and sellers.

**Interior AI — best for prosumers and realtors who want volume.**
A well-known rendering and virtual-staging tool. No shoppable list, no human help, and priced for professionals. *Best for:* people generating lots of renders.

**RoomGPT — best free idea generator.**
One of the original "photo in, restyle out" tools. Fast and cheap for inspiration, but the furniture isn't real and you're on your own from there. *Best for:* brainstorming.

**Havenly & Decorilla — best full human e-design (if budget allows).**
Both pair you with a real designer and hand over a shoppable plan. Excellent quality; the trade-off is price (from ~$199–$299 per room and up) and there's no free AI way to explore first. *Best for:* a whole-room project where you want maximum hand-holding and have the budget.

**IKEA Kreativ & Wayfair Decorify — best free option (with a catch).**
Both let you redesign a room and buy the result — for free. The catch: everything comes from that one store's catalog, so it's shopping, not independent advice. *Best for:* someone already committed to IKEA or Wayfair.

- **Want it done for you, budget no object?** Havenly or Decorilla.

1. **Q:** What is the best AI interior design tool in 2026?
   **A:** It depends on what you need. For quick free ideas, RoomGPT-style tools work. For a shoppable redesign you can buy, MeltFlex or REimagine Home are strong. If you want AI speed plus a real human designer and a curated shopping list without paying $200+ upfront, Designature Studio is built for that middle ground.

2. **Q:** Can AI interior design tools show real furniture I can actually buy?
   **A:** Some can. Tools like Designature Studio, MeltFlex, and REimagine Home attach a list of real, purchasable products to the redesign. Many render-only tools do not — the furniture in the image isn't a real product.

3. **Q:** Are free AI interior design tools any good?
   **A:** Free tiers are great for ideas and inspiration. Their limits are usually realism, a small number of designs, and no human help or shoppable list. Retailer tools like IKEA Kreativ are free but only show that store's products.

4. **Q:** Do any AI interior design tools include a real designer?
   **A:** Most don't. Human e-design services like Havenly and Decorilla do, but start around $199–$299 per room. Designature Studio combines free AI tools with an optional $99 designer consultation that's creditable toward a project.

5. **Q:** Will an AI redesign keep my room's real layout?
   **A:** The better tools analyze your room's geometry and preserve real windows, proportions, and layout. Weaker tools sometimes "hallucinate" new doors or windows, so it's worth checking the result against your actual space.

6. **Q:** How much do AI interior design tools cost in 2026?
   **A:** Free tiers are common. Paid AI tools typically run $15–$50/month; prosumer tools go higher. Full human e-design starts around $199–$299 per room. Designature Studio is free to start, with $19 and $49 tiers and a $99 designer consultation.
