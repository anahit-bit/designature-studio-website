Investor talking points.

Internal — not for investor eyes

Your cheat sheet for investor conversations. Pairs with the Market & Opportunity one-pager (that one you can share; this one you keep). The goal: sound precise, cite defensible numbers, and pre-empt the hard questions before they're asked.

The one-line pitch

"A $1T+ home market, an AI layer growing ~25% a year, and we make money the way the survivors do — on commerce and distribution, with humans as premium margin, not the cost that killed Modsy."

Numbers to use — and to avoid

Cite with confidence

  • Interior design $138B, 4.3% → $176B by 2030 (Grand View)
  • Home décor $960B → $1.6T, 9.4% + furniture ~$700B (Grand View) — the commerce TAM
  • AI interior design as a range: ~$1.5–3.3B → ~$11–15B by 2035, 21–27% CAGR — and name the firms
  • Audience: ~1.4M US Realtors (NAR), 9M+ Airbnb listings
  • UK (primary sources): interior design £1.5B (IBISWorld); online furniture $10.6B, ~40% online (Statista); ~25k estate agencies, ~1.1M sales/yr (HMRC), ~451k short-lets (ONS)
  • Staging ROI: NAR — 49% of agents saw less time on market, 29% saw higher offers
  • Comps: Manycore IPO, Collov $23M, Yardzen exit, Styldod's MLS-backed seed

Avoid / handle with care

  • Don't quote a single precise "AI interior design market = $X" — it's thinly sourced; use the range
  • Don't present a precise virtual-staging TAM (one shaky source) — use the audience + ROI instead
  • Don't imply the core services market is booming — US & UK interior design is flat/declining (IBISWorld)
  • Drop vendor stats ("73% faster / 97% cheaper") and SEO-grade figures — sharp investors will catch them
  • Pre-empt the Modsy question: show your unit economics are AI-led, not designer-cost-led
  • UK: no measured standalone AI-interior market yet — use the ~$1B-by-2033 figure only as a labelled forecast; note UK virtual staging is still emerging vs the US

Answers to the questions they'll ask

"Isn't this just another AI render tool? There are dozens."
Right — the render is a commodity. We monetize what they don't: a shoppable list (we earn on the furniture) and a human-review layer for quality. The moat is commerce + trust, not the model.
"Modsy raised $72M and died. Why are you different?"
Modsy paid human designers as its main cost and burned VC to cover it. We flip that: AI does the cost-heavy work, humans appear only as a paid premium tier, and we take furniture commission — so unit economics stand on their own.
"What's the exit?"
Strategic acquisition — a retailer, proptech, or building-products player (like Yardzen → CRH, or Havenly rolling up brands). IPO is possible only at platform scale (Manycore did it in 2026).
"Where do you win against the well-funded players?"
Two moats they lack: the shoppable loop (Collov/Interior AI stop at a pretty picture) and a human-quality option (pure-AI tools have none). Plus a real-estate virtual-staging wedge via agent/host distribution.
