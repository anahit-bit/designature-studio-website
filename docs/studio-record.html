The Studio Record

Nineteen cards, one record. Every card reads what the ones before it established and writes back what it learned — so a card reached warm never re-derives a number an upstream card already knows, and a card reached cold asks only for what is genuinely missing. This board is the netlist: each arrow is labelled with the facet it carries.

What the visitor sees

One project, dug through card by card

None of the machinery below is visible to the person using it. They answer a short questionnaire, then work through the cards in order — and each one already knows what the last one established. This is the whole product, in eleven moves.

  1. 01Start a projectNot a card A short questionnaire — who you are, where the property is, what you are doing and roughly what you will spend. This is the background a designer would ask for in a first meeting, and every card afterwards reads it.
  2. 02Find My StyleCard Eight taps. Returns your style, and it never has to be asked again.
  3. —Score My Room · skippedNothing to score There is no room yet. The card stays available, but this visitor has no reason to open it — and the studio should not pretend otherwise.
  4. 03Upload your planNot a card We read it: the scale, every room, every door and window. Bare walls are fine — an unfinished shell is the normal Armenian starting point, not an edge case. Then: “We found 6 rooms. Which shall we start with?”
  5. 04Zone My HomeCard What each room is for, and how the home flows between them. It can propose a partition — it will never tell you a wall can come out.
  6. 05Plan My RoomCard Furniture that actually fits, to scale, one room at a time. Never asks for a dimension the plan already gave.
  7. 06Plumb · Light · Heat · WireCards The services, only where the room needs them. Plumb My Room is where the toilet, basin and shower go — and which fixtures fit, built-in or freestanding.
  8. 07Redesign My RoomCard Now you see it — with the layout, the services and the style already decided, instead of guessed.
  9. 08Schedule My FinishesCard Quantities a supplier can price: m² of tile, litres of paint, linear metres of trim.
  10. 09Shop My RoomCard A buyable list — and, because the room is measured, one that can be checked for fit.
  11. 10Cost My ProjectCard The number, built from the list and the quantities rather than from a guess.
  12. 11Phase My RenoCard The order of works, so nothing is tiled before it is wired.
Everything below this line is invisible to them. The record, the provenance ladder, the contracts — none of it appears on screen. It exists so that step 07 does not ask again for what step 03 already established, and so that no card can quietly overwrite a number a better source already knows.

          A question worth settling

          Neither tool models a home. Ingest does.

          “Is it doing a room or a home?” is the right question, and the answer is that it should be neither — because the moment a card has to decide, the same segmentation logic gets written twice. Pull that step out in front of every card and the ambiguity disappears.

          not a card  ·  runs once, before anything

          Does

          Never
          Invents a room, or a dimension.
          So the home is never modelled as a thing. It is a Project holding rooms, plus the adjacencies between them. There is no home object and no home-versus-room mode — separation happens once, at ingest, and everything after it is room by room, forever. The single-room visitor is not a special case: they get a Project with one room in it, down the identical path.
          “One upload, then which room?” is now literal. It is not a fork between two tools — it is ingest handing back n rooms and asking how many of them you want to work on. One is Plan My Room. All of them is Zone My Home first.
          Geometry flows down; the verdict flows back up. Purpose only ever travels Zone → Room. But Plan My Room is the only card that can discover a room cannot do the job it was given — “a cot, a changing table and a 900 mm route do not fit in 2.1 × 2.4 m with the door swinging inwards.” It writes purpose.feasible = false with its reason, and Zone My Home reads the record on its next run. No cycle in the card graph; the loop lives in the data, over time. Point-to-point payloads would have made this the hard case — the record makes it an ordinary read.
          The record

          Three scopes, one envelope

          A flat room object cannot hold both “I have two cats” — true forever — and “this wall is 3,640 mm” — true of one room. So the record nests, and each level carries facets that outlive the card that wrote them.

          Provenance

          How “never re-derive” stops being a note and becomes a type

          Every value in the record is wrapped in a Fact<T> carrying its source, the card that wrote it, and a confidence. writeFact() refuses any write that ranks below the fact already in place — only the person who owns the room may override.

          Two consequences fall out for free: Redesign My Room cannot overwrite a plan’s millimetre with a guess from a photograph, and every number on screen can answer “where did that come from?”

          Today

          Three arrows carry data. All three lose something.

          The next-step band added in AI-032 v2 makes every card navigable to the next. It transfers no state — so a visitor who has just been told their room scores 6/10 on lighting arrives at Redesign with nothing but the photograph.
